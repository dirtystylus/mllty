“Men, amirite?”<br>
— George Eliot, probably

I did not expect that this would be so concerned with medical advancements and land reform in addition to its central focus on the legal and societal constraints imposed on women. Eliot is empathetic but unsparing with her characters, and even the ones she is clearly fond of are not spared tragedy and loss. Like Austen, the romantic plots convey the sheer precarity of womanhood. How is a woman to have a good, fulfilling life, if the men she is forced to depend on are so feckless? I don’t know, George. I don’t know.
