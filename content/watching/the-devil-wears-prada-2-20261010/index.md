Fun to see these people onscreen again, even if it mostly feels like they’ve dispensed with character in favor of fan service. The Miranda Priestly of this movie is diminished in a way that sucks all the fun out of it; the script really fails to give Streep any memorable scenes.

The tension that’s supposed to build around _Runway_’s future never builds because you can’t see the pieces moving; you just get a half-dozen cuts to Hathaway on calls yelling “get her on the phone!” and then things are resolved. It’s like a heist where they keep all of the action offscreen. Sad that the only imaginable salvation (for a time) comes from finding the right benevolent billionaire. Sadder yet is the fact that the media (esp. film) environment has gotten even worse in the six months since this was in theaters.

I suppose there’s a funnier version of this that leans harder into how everything, even fashion, has descended into the same sweaty pit of tap-dancing for algorithmic engagement. But that would probably be too bitter a taste for what is supposed to be escapist nostalgia.

Like many modern productions it looks dull, with no sense of direction to the lighting — even a boat ride across Lake Como lacks a sense of sunlit luxury. Afterwards Jordan and I watched a few minutes of the original to reset our eyes, which was probably the wrong move and made this one feel even worse by comparison.
