Small-scale Soderbergh is still enjoyable. Stronger when it’s dealing with regret and creative stagnation versus intergenerational conflict. McKellen and Coel bounce off each other well — there’s moments where I expected it to get shouty, but the two of them do more with silence and the expression on their faces. Dunning and Corden are game but their scenes are from a different movie, tonally. The plot gets a little confusing/ambiguous at the end, at one point Coel says “it doesn’t really matter” and that feels like a bit of a meta joke. At times the score was David Holmes doing Radiohead, which…no complaints.
